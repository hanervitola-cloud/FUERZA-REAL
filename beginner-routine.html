Beginner Routine: Your First 3 Months

12 min read · Training
← All articles
Written by the creator of Earned Strength · Updated August 2026

The first 3 months at the gym determine whether you'll still be training in a year, or whether you'll end up injured, frustrated, or simply bored. Here's the real plan, month by month.

In summary
  • Full body 3 days per week, with rest between each session
  • 3 sets of 8–12 reps per exercise to start
  • The first month is about learning technique, not lifting heavy
  • Copying advanced routines and jumping from routine to routine are the most common mistakes

The most common beginner mistake isn't the exercise they choose — it's wanting to do too much, too fast, without mastering the basics first. This guide is designed to build a solid foundation: technique, consistency, and progression, in that order.

It took me exactly those first months to understand that technique mattered more than the number on the bar. Early on I wanted to increase weight every week no matter what, and I ended up feeling things in joints I shouldn't have been feeling — nothing serious, but enough to drop the ego and focus on doing it right before doing it heavy.

Muscle-group split — 3 days

This structure is known as "push / pull / legs" and is one of the most widely used — by beginners and experienced trainees alike — because in just 3 days you cover the whole body by grouping muscles that naturally work together.

Day Focus
MondayChest, shoulders and triceps
TuesdayRest
WednesdayBack and biceps
ThursdayRest
FridayFull legs
Saturday / SundayRest
Day 1 — Monday

Chest, shoulders and triceps

  • Bench press — 4 sets × 8–10
  • Overhead press with barbell or dumbbells — 3 sets × 10–12
  • Lateral raises — 3 sets × 12–15
  • Tricep pushdown on cable — 3 sets × 10–12
Day 2 — Wednesday

Back and biceps

  • Lat pulldown (or pull-ups) — 4 sets × 8–10
  • Barbell or machine row — 3 sets × 10–12
  • Barbell bicep curl — 3 sets × 10–12
  • Hammer curl — 2 sets × 12
Day 3 — Friday

Full legs

  • Squat — 4 sets × 8–10
  • Romanian deadlift — 3 sets × 10–12
  • Hip thrust (glutes) — 3 sets × 10–12
  • Leg press or lunges — 3 sets × 12
  • Calf raises — 3 sets × 15

This 3-day structure works well for both beginners and intermediate trainees — each muscle group rests the whole week before repeating, giving more than enough time to recover between sessions.

Core and abs — don't forget them

Core doesn't always fit neatly into a muscle-group split, but it's key for stabilizing everything else. Add this to the end of any of the 3 days, 2–3 times per week:

  • Plank — 3 sets × 30–45 seconds
  • Hanging or lying leg raises — 3 sets × 12–15
  • Crunch or machine ab — 3 sets × 15

Sets and reps for beginners

No need to overcomplicate it: 3 sets of 8 to 12 reps per exercise is the classic starting point with the most support for beginners — enough stimulus without overloading the nervous system while you're learning technique.

Simple rule

If you finish 12 reps easily with 3–4 more "in the tank," increase the weight slightly next session. If you can't reach 8, lower the weight. That's what real progression looks like in practice.

Month by month: what to expect

•

Month 1 — Learn the movement

The goal is not to lift heavy — it's to engrave the correct technique. Feeling awkward with new exercises in the first 2–3 weeks is normal. That's your nervous system learning the pattern, not a lack of strength.

•

Month 2 — Real progression begins

With technique more comfortable, you start consistently adding weight week by week. This is usually when the first noticeable strength changes appear.

•

Month 3 — Consistency and adjustments

You have a base now. Time to review which exercises feel right, which ones need more practice, and start gradually increasing volume (sets) if the body is ready for it.

Most common mistakes in the first year

01

Copying an advanced person's routine

What works for someone with 3 years of training isn't what someone just starting needs. Start simple, add complexity later.

02

Switching routines every 2 weeks

Jumping from routine to routine doesn't give your body time to adapt or progress in any specific movement.

03

Training without rest between sessions

As we covered in the myths article: muscle grows when it rests, not when you train every day without a break.

04

Increasing weight before mastering technique

Ego is the number one enemy of long-term progress. Prioritize correct movement over the number on the bar.
