Why Using Steroids Isn't Worth It

9 min read · Training & Health
← All articles
Written by the creator of Earned Strength · Updated August 2026

Building muscle fast sounds tempting, but anabolic steroids aren't a free shortcut — they send your body a bill, and often it's a permanent one.

In summary
  • Steroids speed up muscle gain, but have real effects on your heart, liver, and hormones
  • Several effects (gynecomastia, hair loss, virilization) can be permanent
  • When you come off the cycle, much of the muscle gained is lost as hormone levels crash
  • The natural path is slower, but it doesn't take anything back from you afterward

When someone starts at the gym and sees slow results, it's natural to look for ways to speed up the process. That's where anabolic steroids show up as an easy promise. The problem is that what they promise short-term, they charge for long-term — and in some cases the bill is irreversible.

What they actually do to your body

01

Heart and cardiovascular system

They raise blood pressure and bad cholesterol (LDL), which increases the risk of a heart attack even in young, apparently healthy people.

02

Liver and kidneys under stress

These organs process the substance. With prolonged use, the liver can develop structural damage and the kidneys lose efficiency filtering the blood.

03

Your body stops producing its own testosterone

When it receives external testosterone, your testes detect that "there's already enough" and slow down or stop their own production. This doesn't recover overnight.

04

Physical changes that don't go away

Gynecomastia (growth of breast tissue in men), severe acne, premature hair loss. In women, virilization: deeper voice and facial hair growth — changes that in many cases are permanent.

05

Mental health and aggression

Sudden mood swings, irritability and aggression — what's popularly known as "roid rage" — plus the real risk of psychological dependence on the substance.

06

The crash after the cycle

When you stop using them, your natural testosterone production takes time to recover. During that period, you lose a large part of the muscle you gained — exactly what the red curve shows in performance charts.

07

It's illegal without a prescription

In most countries, buying and using them without a prescription is illegal. What's obtained through unofficial channels also has no quality control or guaranteed safe dosage.

The real comparison

Natural muscle grows more slowly, but it stays. Steroid muscle rises fast and a significant portion is lost as soon as you stop the cycle — not counting the damage that can remain in your body long after the muscle is gone.

So, what does actually work?

Consistent progressive overload, good sleep, eating enough protein, and above all, patience. There's no shortcut that beats time without charging something in return. The natural path is slower because it's not borrowing from your future health.

How much muscle you can gain naturally (realistic numbers)

One of the biggest myths is thinking that without substances it's impossible to look good. The reality, according to exercise physiology evidence, is this:

•

First year of serious training

A well-fed beginner with a good routine can gain between 9 and 11 kg of muscle in the first year. It's the period of greatest progress in your entire training life.

•

Second and third year

The rate roughly halves. The body has adapted and you need to be more precise with technique, rest, and nutrition.

•

After the third year

Gains become slow and are measured in grams, not kilos. This is where many people get frustrated and look for shortcuts — exactly when the risk of turning to substances is highest.

Warning signs if someone close to you is using

If you have a friend or family member training and you notice odd changes, these are signs worth having a conversation about (not judging — talking):

  • Abnormally fast muscle gain (more than the body allows naturally)
  • Sudden acne on back and shoulders in someone who didn't have it before
  • Aggressive mood changes that weren't part of their usual personality
  • Water retention, puffier face
  • Secrecy around "supplements" they don't want to show or name
Effect Does it reverse when you stop?
Blood pressure and cholesterol Yes, usually improves within a few months
Natural testosterone production Recovers, but can take 4 months to over a year
Gynecomastia (breast tissue) Not always — sometimes requires surgery
Severe acne Improves, but can leave permanent scarring
Accelerated hair loss No — once the genetic predisposition is triggered, it won't grow back on its own
Virilization in women (voice, facial hair) No — usually permanent

This is the part that gets talked about least: many of the "temporary" risks actually aren't. That's why the decision shouldn't be made only thinking about what the mirror shows short-term.

Legal alternatives that actually help

If you're looking to speed up results without risking your health, these have real scientific backing:

01

Creatine monohydrate

One of the most studied and safest supplements that exists. It helps with strength and recovery, without the hormonal risks.

02

Enough protein in your diet

Without sufficient protein (approx. 1.6–2.2g per kg of bodyweight), it doesn't matter how much you train — muscle won't build the same way.

03

Quality sleep

Most of the muscle recovery and natural hormone production happens while you sleep. Poor sleep sabotages progress more than almost anything else.
